## Email Folders (Mailbox Structure)

Every email account has a set of standard folders that help organize your messages. Think of these folders like different trays on a desk — each tray holds a different type of mail.

### Standard Email Folders

| Folder | Purpose | Icon |
|--------|---------|------|
| **Inbox** | Received emails appear here | Envelope/tray icon |
| **Sent** (Sent Mail) | Copies of emails you have sent | Paper plane icon |
| **Drafts** | Emails you started but did not send | Pencil/document icon |
| **Spam** (Junk) | Unwanted or suspicious emails filtered by the system | Warning/exclamation icon |
| **Trash** (Bin) | Deleted emails (kept for 30 days in Gmail) | Dustbin icon |
| **Starred** | Emails you have marked as important | Star icon |
| **Important** | Emails automatically marked as important by Gmail | Arrow/tag icon |
| **All Mail** | Every email (including archived) in one place | Folder icon |

**CCC Exam Tip:** "Emails that have been composed but not yet sent are stored in ___" — Answer: **Drafts folder**. "Deleted emails are moved to ___" — Answer: **Trash/Bin folder**. These are very commonly asked.

## Understanding Each Folder in Detail

### Inbox
- This is the **most important folder** — it is the first thing you see when you open Gmail
- All new emails from other people appear in the Inbox
- Gmail categorizes your Inbox into tabs: **Primary**, **Social**, **Promotions**, **Updates**
  - **Primary:** Personal and important emails
  - **Social:** Notifications from Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, etc.
  - **Promotions:** Marketing emails, offers, discounts
  - **Updates:** Receipts, bills, bank notifications

### Sent Mail (Sent Items)
- Every email you send is automatically saved in the Sent folder
- This serves as a **record** of your outgoing communication
- You can re-read what you sent to someone by checking this folder
- You **cannot edit** emails in the Sent folder — they are read-only copies

### Drafts
- When you start writing an email but close the compose window without sending, it is saved as a Draft
- Gmail **auto-saves** drafts every few seconds while you type
- You can open a draft, continue editing, and then send it when ready
- Once you send a draft, it moves from Drafts to Sent

### Spam (Junk Mail)
- Gmail's spam filter automatically moves suspicious emails to this folder
- Spam includes: advertisements you did not subscribe to, phishing attempts, scam offers
- Emails in Spam are **automatically deleted after 30 days**
- You can manually mark an email as "Not Spam" if it was incorrectly filtered
- You can also report genuine spam emails by clicking the **Report Spam** button

### Trash (Bin)
- When you delete an email, it moves to the Trash folder (it is not permanently deleted immediately)
- Emails in Trash are **automatically deleted permanently after 30 days** in Gmail
- You can recover deleted emails from Trash within 30 days
- To permanently delete immediately, go to Trash and click **Empty Trash Now**

**CCC Exam Tip:** Both Spam and Trash are auto-cleared after **30 days** in Gmail. This timeframe is frequently asked.

## Sorting and Searching Emails

### Searching Emails
Gmail has a powerful search bar at the top of the page. You can search by:

| Search Method | Example | What It Finds |
|--------------|---------|---------------|
| By sender | `from:priya@gmail.com` | All emails from Priya |
| By recipient | `to:rahul@gmail.com` | All emails sent to Rahul |
| By subject | `subject:meeting` | Emails with "meeting" in subject |
| By keyword | `project report` | Emails containing those words |
| By attachment | `has:attachment` | All emails with attachments |
| By date | `before:2026/07/01` | Emails before July 1, 2026 |
| By label | `label:work` | Emails tagged with "work" label |
| Unread only | `is:unread` | All unread emails |

**CCC Exam Tip:** Searching emails is an important skill. The search operators like `from:`, `to:`, `subject:`, and `has:attachment` are useful to know for the exam.

### Sorting Emails
While Gmail does not have a traditional "sort" button like a spreadsheet, you can organize emails by:
- Using the **search bar** with operators
- Using **filters** to automatically categorize incoming emails
- Using **labels** (explained below) to tag emails by category

## Starring and Flagging Emails

### Stars in Gmail
- Click the **star icon** next to any email to mark it as important
- Starred emails appear in the **Starred** folder for quick access
- You can use different colored stars (yellow, blue, red, green, purple, orange)
- Stars help you find important emails quickly without searching

### When to Star an Email
- Important messages you need to follow up on
- Emails with deadlines or action items
- Reference emails you may need to find quickly later

**Example:** Ananya receives an email about her CCC exam date. She stars it so she can quickly find it later.

## Labels and Folders

### What are Labels?
Labels in Gmail are like **colored tags** that you can attach to emails. They work similar to folders but are more flexible because one email can have multiple labels.

### Creating a Label
1. Click the **gear icon** (Settings) in Gmail
2. Go to **Labels** tab
3. Click **Create New Label**
4. Enter a name (e.g., "CCC Study", "Work", "Personal")
5. Click **Create**

### Using Labels
- You can drag and drop emails into labels
- You can right-click an email and select "Label as"
- Labels appear in the left sidebar of Gmail
- Click a label to see all emails with that label

### Examples of Useful Labels

| Label Name | Purpose |
|-----------|---------|
| CCC Study | All emails related to CCC exam preparation |
| Work | Office-related emails |
| Personal | Family and friends' emails |
| Bills | Electricity, phone, internet bills |
| Important Docs | Documents to keep for reference |

## Filters and Automatic Organization

Gmail allows you to create **filters** that automatically organize incoming emails.

### Creating a Filter
1. Click the **search bar** dropdown arrow
2. Enter criteria (e.g., From: newsletter@example.com)
3. Click **Create filter**
4. Choose actions:
   - Skip the Inbox (Archive it)
   - Apply a label (e.g., "Newsletters")
   - Mark as read
   - Delete it
   - Forward to another email
5. Click **Create filter**

**Example:** Amit creates a filter so all emails from his college (ending in @college.edu.in) automatically get the label "College" and are marked as important.

## Contacts (Address Book)

### What is an Address Book?
The **address book** (or Contacts) is where you store email addresses and contact information of people you communicate with frequently.

### Accessing Contacts in Gmail
- Click the **Google Apps** menu (9 dots icon) in the top right
- Select **Contacts**
- Or visit: `contacts.google.com`

### Adding a Contact
1. Open Google Contacts
2. Click **Create contact**
3. Enter details: Name, Email, Phone number, Company
4. Click **Save**

### Benefits of Using Contacts
- **Auto-complete:** When you start typing a name in the To field, Gmail suggests matching contacts
- **Groups:** You can create contact groups (e.g., "CCC Classmates") and email the entire group at once
- **Import/Export:** You can import contacts from a CSV file or export your contacts for backup

**CCC Exam Tip:** The address book stores email addresses for quick access. Gmail auto-suggests contacts when you start typing in the To, CC, or BCC fields.

## Archiving Emails

**Archiving** means removing an email from your Inbox without deleting it. The email is still in your account (in **All Mail**) but does not clutter your Inbox.

- Click the **Archive** button (box with down arrow) or press the **E** key
- Archived emails can be found in **All Mail**
- If someone replies to an archived email, it reappears in your Inbox
