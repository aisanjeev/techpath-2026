## What is Netiquette?

**Netiquette** is short for **Network Etiquette** (or **Internet Etiquette**). It refers to the set of informal rules and guidelines for polite and respectful behavior when communicating on the internet.

Just as there are manners for face-to-face conversations (like saying "please" and "thank you"), there are manners for online communication. Following netiquette makes the internet a better place for everyone.

**CCC Exam Tip:** Netiquette = Network + Etiquette = Internet manners. This definition is commonly asked in the CCC exam.

## Rules of Netiquette

### General Internet Etiquette Rules

1. **Be Polite and Respectful**
   - Use courteous language in emails, messages, and social media
   - Treat others online the way you would treat them in person
   - Avoid personal attacks, insults, or offensive language

2. **Do Not Type in ALL CAPITALS**
   - Writing in ALL CAPS is considered **SHOUTING** on the internet
   - Example: "SEND ME THE FILE NOW!!!" sounds aggressive
   - Better: "Could you please send me the file?" sounds polite

3. **Think Before You Post**
   - Once something is posted online, it can be very difficult to remove
   - Do not share private information about others without their permission
   - Do not forward personal emails without the sender's consent

4. **Respect Others' Privacy**
   - Do not share someone else's email address, phone number, or photos without permission
   - Use BCC when sending mass emails to protect recipients' email addresses
   - Do not read other people's emails or messages without permission

5. **Use Proper Grammar and Spelling**
   - Write clearly so your message is understood
   - Avoid excessive use of slang or abbreviations in professional emails
   - Proofread your message before sending

6. **Do Not Spam**
   - Do not send unnecessary bulk messages to people
   - Do not forward chain emails ("Forward this to 10 people or bad luck will follow")
   - Unsubscribe from newsletters you no longer want instead of ignoring them

7. **Give Credit to Original Authors**
   - If you share someone's content, give them credit
   - Do not claim others' work as your own (plagiarism)

8. **Be Careful with Humor and Sarcasm**
   - Written messages lack tone of voice and facial expressions
   - Jokes or sarcasm can easily be misunderstood
   - Use emoticons/emojis to clarify tone when appropriate (in informal messages)

9. **Respond in a Timely Manner**
   - Reply to emails within 24-48 hours
   - If you need more time, send a brief acknowledgment saying you will respond soon

10. **Follow Platform-Specific Rules**
    - Each platform (email, social media, forums) may have its own community guidelines
    - Read and follow the rules of the platform you are using

**CCC Exam Tip:** "Typing in ALL CAPS on the internet means ___" — Answer: **Shouting**. This is one of the most frequently asked netiquette questions.

## Spam — Unwanted Emails

### What is Spam?
**Spam** refers to **unsolicited bulk emails** — unwanted messages sent to a large number of people without their consent. Spam is the email equivalent of junk mail in your physical mailbox.

### Types of Spam
| Type | Description | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Advertising spam | Unwanted product promotions | "Buy watches at 90% discount!" |
| Phishing emails | Fake emails pretending to be from trusted sources | "Your bank account is locked, click here" |
| Chain emails | Messages asking you to forward to others | "Forward to 10 friends for good luck" |
| Scam emails | Fraudulent offers | "You have won ₹50 lakhs, send your details" |
| Malware emails | Emails with infected attachments | "Open this attachment for free software" |

### How to Handle Spam
- **Do not open** suspicious emails from unknown senders
- **Do not click** links in spam emails
- **Do not reply** to spam (it confirms your email is active)
- **Do not download** attachments from spam emails
- **Mark as spam** — click the "Report Spam" button in Gmail
- **Unsubscribe** from legitimate marketing emails you no longer want
- **Use spam filters** — Gmail's spam filter automatically catches most spam

**CCC Exam Tip:** Spam is **unsolicited bulk email**. The best action when you receive spam is to **mark it as spam and delete it** — never click links or open attachments.

## Phishing — How to Spot Fake Emails

### What is Phishing?
**Phishing** is a type of cyber attack where criminals send fake emails that **look like they are from trusted organizations** (banks, government, companies) to trick you into revealing personal information like passwords, credit card numbers, or bank details.

The word "phishing" comes from "fishing" — criminals cast a wide net of fake emails hoping someone will "bite."

### How to Identify a Phishing Email

| Warning Sign | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| Unknown or suspicious sender | support@bank-secure-login.xyz |
| Generic greeting | "Dear Customer" instead of your name |
| Urgent language | "Your account will be CLOSED in 24 hours!" |
| Spelling and grammar mistakes | "Youre acount has been compramised" |
| Suspicious links | Link text says "www.sbi.co.in" but actual URL is different |
| Request for personal information | "Enter your ATM PIN, password, and OTP" |
| Too good to be true offers | "You won ₹1 Crore! Claim now!" |
| Threatening language | "Legal action will be taken if you don't respond" |

### Examples of Phishing Messages

**Phishing Email Example:**
> From: security@sbi-alerts-india.com
> Subject: URGENT: Your SBI Account Has Been Blocked!
> Dear Valued Customer,
> Your account has been temporarily blocked due to suspicious activity. Please click the link below and verify your details within 24 hours to restore access.
> [Click Here to Verify]

**Why this is phishing:**
- SBI's real domain is `sbi.co.in`, not `sbi-alerts-india.com`
- Uses generic "Dear Valued Customer" instead of your name
- Creates urgency with "24 hours"
- Asks you to click a link and enter personal details
- Real banks **never** ask for passwords or PINs via email

### How to Protect Yourself from Phishing
1. **Never click** links in suspicious emails
2. **Check the sender's email address** carefully — look for misspellings
3. **Hover over links** (without clicking) to see the real URL
4. **Contact the organization directly** using their official website or phone number
5. **Never share** passwords, OTP, ATM PIN, or CVV via email
6. **Report phishing** emails to the email service and the organization being impersonated
7. **Enable two-factor authentication** (2FA) on important accounts

**CCC Exam Tip:** Remember that banks and government agencies **never** ask for passwords, PINs, or OTPs via email. Any email requesting such information is likely phishing.

## Instant Messaging (IM)

### What is Instant Messaging?
**Instant Messaging (IM)** is a form of real-time text communication between two or more people over the internet. Unlike email, instant messages are delivered and received **instantly**, allowing live conversation.

### Popular Instant Messaging Apps in India

| App | Developer | Key Features |
|-----|-----------|-------------|
| **WhatsApp** | Meta (Facebook) | Text, voice calls, video calls, file sharing, groups |
| **Telegram** | Telegram Messenger | Large groups, channels, bots, large file sharing |
| **Signal** | Signal Foundation | End-to-end encryption, privacy-focused |
| **Google Chat** | Google | Integrated with Gmail and Google Workspace |
| **Facebook Messenger** | Meta | Text, calls, stories, mini-games |

### Features of Instant Messaging
1. **Real-time communication** — Messages are delivered instantly
2. **Read receipts** — See when your message has been read (blue ticks in WhatsApp)
3. **Group chats** — Communicate with multiple people at once
4. **File sharing** — Share photos, videos, documents, and voice notes
5. **Voice and video calls** — Make free calls over the internet
6. **Status updates** — Share stories or status updates with contacts
7. **End-to-end encryption** — Messages are secured so only sender and receiver can read them

### Email vs Instant Messaging

| Feature | Email | Instant Messaging |
|---------|-------|-------------------|
| Speed | Fast (seconds to minutes) | Instant (real-time) |
| Formality | Formal and professional | Informal and casual |
| Message length | Can be long and detailed | Usually short messages |
| Attachments | Up to 25 MB (Gmail) | Varies by app (WhatsApp: 2 GB) |
| Best for | Official communication, records | Quick conversations, coordination |
| Read receipts | Optional | Built-in (blue ticks) |
| Group communication | CC/BCC | Group chats |

## Using Emoticons and Emojis

### What are Emoticons?
**Emoticons** are text-based representations of facial expressions made using keyboard characters.

| Emoticon | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| :-) or :) | Happy, smiling |
| :-( or :( | Sad, unhappy |
| ;-) or ;) | Winking |
| :-D or :D | Laughing |
| :-P or :P | Tongue out, playful |
| :-O or :O | Surprised |
| <3 | Heart, love |

### What are Emojis?
**Emojis** are small digital images or icons used to express emotions, ideas, or objects in electronic messages. They evolved from emoticons and are now standardized across devices.

### When to Use Emoticons/Emojis
- **Appropriate:** Personal messages, group chats, casual communication with friends
- **Avoid:** Formal emails, professional communication, official documents, job applications

**CCC Exam Tip:** Emoticons are text-based (made from keyboard characters like `:-)`) while emojis are graphical icons. Avoid using them in formal or professional emails.

## Professional Email Writing Tips

### Structure of a Professional Email

```
To: professor.sharma@university.ac.in
Subject: Request for Extension on Assignment Deadline

Respected Professor Sharma,

I hope this email finds you well.

I am writing to request a two-day extension on the CCC Module 7 
assignment, which is currently due on July 28, 2026. I have been 
unwell this week and was unable to complete the work on time.

I would be grateful if you could extend the deadline to July 30, 2026. 
I assure you that I will submit the completed assignment by then.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Regards,
Priya Verma
Roll No: CCC-2026-042
TechPath Institute, Bhopal
```

### Tips for Professional Emails
1. **Use a clear subject line** — Be specific about the purpose
2. **Use a proper greeting** — "Respected Sir/Ma'am", "Dear Mr./Ms."
3. **Keep it concise** — Get to the point quickly
4. **Use proper grammar** — No slang or text abbreviations
5. **Be polite** — Use "please", "thank you", "kindly"
6. **Include a signature** — Your name, designation, and contact information
7. **Proofread** — Check for spelling and grammar errors before sending
8. **Avoid attachments unless necessary** — Mention what you are attaching
