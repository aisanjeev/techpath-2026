## Composing an Email

**Composing** an email means writing a new email message. In Gmail, you click the **Compose** button (usually a large button with a "+" or pencil icon) to start writing a new email.

### Parts of a New Email Window

When you click Compose in Gmail, a new window appears with these fields:

| Field | Purpose | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **To** | Main recipient's email address | priya@gmail.com |
| **CC** | Carbon Copy — additional recipients | amit@gmail.com |
| **BCC** | Blind Carbon Copy — hidden recipients | sneha@gmail.com |
| **Subject** | Brief description of the email topic | "Meeting on Monday" |
| **Body** | The main message content | Your actual message |
| **Attach** | Button to add files | Click the paperclip icon |

**CCC Exam Tip:** Know all the fields in a compose window. The **To** field is mandatory — you must enter at least one recipient. The **Subject** field is optional but strongly recommended.

## To, CC, and BCC — Understanding the Difference

This is one of the most important topics for the CCC exam. Understanding when to use To, CC, and BCC is essential.

### To (Direct Recipient)
- The **primary recipient** of your email
- The person who needs to read and possibly respond to the email
- You can add multiple email addresses in the To field
- **Example:** You are sending a project report to your teacher — put your teacher's email in the **To** field

### CC (Carbon Copy)
- **CC** stands for **Carbon Copy**
- Used when you want to send a **copy** of the email to someone for their information
- CC recipients can see everyone else who received the email
- The CC recipient is not the primary audience but should be aware of the communication
- **Example:** You send a leave application to your boss (To) and put your team leader in CC so they are informed

### BCC (Blind Carbon Copy)
- **BCC** stands for **Blind Carbon Copy**
- Used when you want to send a copy to someone **without other recipients knowing**
- BCC recipients are **hidden** — no one else can see their email address
- BCC recipients can see the To and CC recipients, but not other BCC recipients
- **Example:** You send a newsletter to 500 customers — put all addresses in BCC to protect everyone's privacy

### Comparison Table

| Feature | To | CC | BCC |
|---------|-----|-----|------|
| Full form | (Direct recipient) | Carbon Copy | Blind Carbon Copy |
| Purpose | Primary recipient | For information | Hidden copy |
| Visible to others? | Yes | Yes | No |
| Can see other recipients? | Yes | Yes | Cannot see other BCC |
| Common use | Main audience | Keep someone informed | Mass emails, privacy |

**CCC Exam Tip:** "CC stands for ___" (Carbon Copy) and "BCC recipients are visible to ___" (No one / Only the sender) are very frequently asked. Also remember: **CC recipients are visible to all; BCC recipients are hidden from all**.

## Sending an Email

After composing your email, follow these steps to send it:

1. Enter the recipient's email address in the **To** field
2. Add CC or BCC recipients if needed
3. Type a clear **Subject** line
4. Write your message in the **Body** area
5. Attach files if needed (click the paperclip icon)
6. Click the **Send** button

Once sent, the email moves to your **Sent** folder automatically.

**Important:** Once an email is sent, you **cannot unsend it** (Gmail offers a brief "Undo Send" window of 5-30 seconds, but after that, the email is permanently sent).

## Replying to an Email

When someone sends you an email and you want to respond:

### Reply
- Click the **Reply** button (curved arrow icon) at the bottom of the email
- Your response goes only to the **original sender**
- The original email is included below your reply for reference
- The subject line automatically gets "Re:" added in front (e.g., "Re: Meeting on Monday")

### Reply All
- Click **Reply All** to respond to the sender AND all CC recipients
- Use this when everyone in the conversation needs to see your response
- Be careful with Reply All — only use it when all recipients need your reply

**CCC Exam Tip:** **Reply** sends to the original sender only. **Reply All** sends to the sender and all CC recipients. "Re:" in the subject line indicates a reply.

## Forwarding an Email

**Forwarding** means sending an email you received to someone else.

- Click the **Forward** button
- Enter the new recipient's email address in the **To** field
- You can add your own message above the forwarded content
- The subject line gets "Fwd:" added in front (e.g., "Fwd: Meeting on Monday")
- All attachments from the original email are included in the forwarded email

**Example:** Rahul receives an important circular from his college. He forwards it to his friend Amit who was absent that day.

**CCC Exam Tip:** "Fwd:" in the subject line indicates a forwarded email. Forwarding sends the entire email (including attachments) to a new person.

## Subject Line

The **Subject** line is a brief description of what your email is about. It appears in the recipient's inbox alongside your name.

### Tips for Writing Good Subject Lines
- Keep it short and clear (5-8 words)
- Be specific about the topic
- Avoid all caps (looks like shouting)
- Avoid leaving it blank

### Examples of Good vs Bad Subject Lines

| Bad Subject | Good Subject |
|-------------|-------------|
| (blank) | Leave Application - 28 July 2026 |
| Hi | Project Report Submission - CCC Module 7 |
| URGENT!!!! | Meeting Rescheduled to 3 PM Today |
| Hello Sir | Question About Assignment Deadline |

## Sending Attachments

An **attachment** is a file that you send along with your email. You can attach documents, images, PDFs, spreadsheets, and more.

### How to Attach a File in Gmail
1. Click the **Compose** button to start a new email
2. Click the **paperclip icon** (Attach files) at the bottom of the compose window
3. Browse your computer and select the file
4. The file uploads and appears as a small box below the subject line
5. Click **Send**

### Attachment Size Limits

| Email Service | Maximum Attachment Size |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Gmail | **25 MB** |
| Yahoo Mail | 25 MB |
| Outlook | 20 MB |

**CCC Exam Tip:** "Maximum attachment size in Gmail is ___" is a very frequently asked question. Answer: **25 MB**. If your file is larger than 25 MB, Gmail automatically uses **Google Drive** to share the file instead.

### Types of Files You Can Attach
- Documents: `.pdf`, `.docx`, `.txt`, `.xlsx`, `.pptx`
- Images: `.jpg`, `.png`, `.gif`, `.bmp`
- Compressed files: `.zip`, `.rar`
- Audio/Video: `.mp3`, `.mp4` (within size limits)

**Note:** Gmail blocks certain file types for security reasons, including `.exe` (executable files), `.bat`, and `.cmd` files.

## Saving as Draft

If you start writing an email but are not ready to send it yet:

- Gmail **automatically saves** your email as a **Draft**
- You can find it in the **Drafts** folder
- Open the draft later to continue writing and then send it
- Drafts are saved on the server, so you can access them from any device
