## Week 11: How Search Engines Work & Keyword Research

### How Search Engines Work

Search engines work in 3 stages:

#### 1. Crawling
- Search engines send "spiders" or "bots" (like Googlebot) to discover new and updated pages
- Bots follow links from one page to another
- They read the code (HTML, CSS, JavaScript) of every page they find
- Crawl budget = how many pages Google will crawl on your site in a given time

#### 2. Indexing
- After crawling, Google stores (indexes) the page content in a massive database
- Google decides what the page is about (topic, keywords, entities)
- Not all crawled pages get indexed — low quality or duplicate content may be skipped
- You can check indexing status in Google Search Console

#### 3. Ranking
- When a user types a query, Google searches its index
- It ranks results based on 200+ factors
- The goal: show the most relevant, useful, and trustworthy result first

**Simple analogy:** Think of Google as a library. Crawling = librarian discovering new books. Indexing = cataloguing books by topic. Ranking = recommending the best book when someone asks a question.

---

### The Big 6 Ranking Factors

While Google uses 200+ signals, these 6 carry the most weight:

| # | Factor | What It Means | How to Optimize |
|---|--------|---------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Content Quality | Helpful, original, in-depth content | Write comprehensive content that answers user intent |
| 2 | Backlinks | Other websites linking to you | Earn links from relevant, authoritative sites |
| 3 | Search Intent Match | Your page matches what the user wants | Analyze SERP to understand intent (informational/transactional/navigational) |
| 4 | Page Experience | Fast, mobile-friendly, secure | Optimize Core Web Vitals, use HTTPS, mobile-responsive design |
| 5 | E-E-A-T | Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness | Show credentials, cite sources, get mentioned by authorities |
| 6 | Freshness | How recently content was updated | Regularly update and refresh content |

### Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) for AI Overviews

With Google's AI Overviews and ChatGPT search, traditional SEO is evolving:

- **What is AEO?** Optimizing your content to appear in AI-generated answers (Google AI Overview, Bing Chat, ChatGPT)
- **How AI picks sources:** Clear structure, direct answers, authoritative content, proper schema markup
- **Tips for AEO:**
  - Answer questions directly in the first 2-3 sentences
  - Use "What is X?" and "How to Y?" headings
  - Structure content with tables, lists, and step-by-step formats
  - Add FAQ schema markup
  - Build topical authority (cover a topic from every angle)

**Try This:** Open Google in incognito mode and search for "how to learn digital marketing." Observe: (1) Is there an AI Overview at the top? What sources does it cite? (2) What type of content ranks in the top 5 — blogs, videos, course pages? (3) Check the "People Also Ask" section and note 3 questions. Now search the same query on ChatGPT or Perplexity — compare the answer and sources cited. This shows you how traditional SEO and AEO differ in practice.

### Keyword Research

#### Key Metrics

| Metric | What It Means | Good Range (for beginners) |
|--------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Search Volume | How many times people search this term per month | 100-10,000 (sweet spot for new sites) |
| Keyword Difficulty (KD) | How hard it is to rank on page 1 (0-100) | Target KD < 30 for new sites |
| CPC (Cost Per Click) | What advertisers pay per click in Google Ads | Higher CPC = more commercial value |
| Search Intent | Why the person is searching | Match your content type to intent |

#### Search Intent Types

| Intent | User Wants | Example Query | Best Content Type |
|--------|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Informational | Learn something | "what is SEO" | Blog post, guide |
| Navigational | Find a specific site | "TechPath Academy login" | Homepage, login page |
| Transactional | Buy something | "buy SEO course online" | Product/service page |
| Commercial Investigation | Compare before buying | "best SEO courses in India" | Comparison post, review |

#### Keyword Research Process

1. **Seed keywords** — Start with 5-10 broad terms related to your niche
2. **Expand** — Use tools (Google Keyword Planner, Ubersuggest) to find related terms
3. **Filter** — Remove irrelevant keywords, group by intent
4. **Prioritize** — Pick keywords with good volume + low difficulty + matching intent
5. **Map** — Assign each keyword to a specific page on your site

---

### Long-Tail vs Short-Tail Keywords

| Feature | Short-Tail | Long-Tail |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Length | 1-2 words | 3+ words |
| Example | "digital marketing" | "digital marketing course in Bhopal for freshers" |
| Volume | Very high (50K+) | Lower (100-1000) |
| Competition | Extremely high | Low to medium |
| Conversion Rate | Low (broad intent) | High (specific intent) |
| Best For | Brand awareness | Targeted traffic & conversions |

**Strategy:** Target long-tail keywords first (easier to rank), then go after short-tail as your site grows.

### Keyword Mapping & Topical Clusters

**Topical Cluster Model:**
- **Pillar Page** — One broad, comprehensive page (e.g., "Complete Guide to Digital Marketing")
- **Cluster Pages** — Multiple detailed pages on subtopics (e.g., "SEO basics," "social media marketing," "email marketing")
- **Internal Links** — All cluster pages link to the pillar page and vice versa

**Benefits:**
- Shows Google you're an authority on the entire topic
- Helps users navigate related content
- Improves internal link structure

**Example Cluster for TechPath:**
- Pillar: "Digital Marketing Career Guide 2026"
- Clusters: "SEO skills for beginners," "Social media jobs in India," "Google Ads certification worth it?"

## Week 12: On-Page SEO

### On-Page SEO Elements Checklist

#### Title Tag
- **What:** The clickable headline in search results
- **Formula:** Primary Keyword | Secondary Keyword | Brand Name
- **Length:** 50-60 characters (Google truncates after ~60)
- **Rules:**
  - Put primary keyword near the beginning
  - Make it compelling (include numbers, power words)
  - Every page needs a unique title tag
- **Example:** `Digital Marketing Course in Bhopal | 100% Placement | TechPath Academy`

#### Meta Description
- **What:** The summary text below the title in search results
- **Length:** 150-155 characters
- **Rules:**
  - Include primary keyword naturally
  - Add a clear CTA (Call to Action)
  - Make it specific (include numbers, benefits)
  - Google may rewrite it, but still write a good one
- **Example:** `Join TechPath's 6-month Digital Marketing course. Learn SEO, Ads, AI tools. 500+ students placed. Starts Jan 2026. Enroll now!`

#### URL Structure
- **Good:** `techpath.biz/digital-marketing-course-bhopal`
- **Bad:** `techpath.biz/page?id=123&cat=5`
- **Rules:**
  - Use hyphens, not underscores
  - Keep it short (3-5 words)
  - Include primary keyword
  - Use lowercase only
  - No special characters or numbers (unless meaningful)

#### Heading Structure (H1-H6)
```
H1: Main Topic (only ONE per page)
  H2: Major Section
    H3: Subsection
      H4: Sub-subsection (rarely needed)
```
- H1 = page title, include primary keyword
- H2 = major sections, include secondary keywords
- H3 = subtopics within H2 sections
- Never skip levels (don't go H1 → H3)

#### Content Optimization

| Concept | What It Means | How to Apply |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| Keyword Density | % of times keyword appears vs total words | Keep 1-2% (don't stuff) |
| LSI Keywords | Related terms Google expects to see | Use synonyms and related words naturally |
| Semantic SEO | Covering the full meaning/context of a topic | Answer all related questions in one piece |
| Entity SEO | Optimizing for things (people, places, brands) | Mention entities clearly, use schema markup |

#### Image SEO
- **File name:** Use descriptive names (`digital-marketing-classroom-bhopal.jpg` not `IMG_2847.jpg`)
- **Alt text:** Describe what's in the image + keyword if natural
- **Size:** Compress images (target < 200KB), use WebP format
- **Dimensions:** Specify width and height to prevent layout shift
- **Lazy loading:** Add `loading="lazy"` for images below the fold

#### Internal Linking
- Link from high-authority pages to newer pages
- Use descriptive anchor text (not "click here")
- Every page should have 3-5 internal links minimum
- Create a logical hierarchy (homepage → category → individual pages)
- Fix orphan pages (pages with no internal links pointing to them)

**Try This:** Go to any 3 websites you visit regularly. Right-click → View Page Source → find the `<title>` tag and `<meta name="description">` tag. For each site, write down: (1) The title tag — does it follow the formula Primary Keyword | Secondary Keyword | Brand Name? Is it 50-60 chars? (2) The meta description — does it have a CTA? Is it 150-155 chars? (3) Check the URL structure — is it clean with hyphens, or messy with parameters? Rate each site out of 10 for on-page SEO.

### Schema Markup (JSON-LD)

Schema markup is code you add to your page to help Google understand your content better. It can give you rich results (stars, FAQs, recipes, etc. in search results).

#### Common Schema Types

| Schema Type | Use Case | Rich Result |
|-------------|----------|-------------|
| LocalBusiness | Physical store/office | Business info in Knowledge Panel |
| Article | Blog posts, news | Article snippet with date, author |
| FAQ | Question-answer content | Expandable Q&A in search results |
| Product | E-commerce products | Price, availability, ratings |
| HowTo | Step-by-step guides | Step-by-step display in search |
| Course | Online courses | Course info with provider |
| Event | Events, workshops | Event date, location in search |

#### JSON-LD Example: LocalBusiness

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "TechPath Academy Bhopal",
  "description": "IT Training Institute offering Digital Marketing, Web Development, and Data Science courses",
  "url": "https://techpath.biz",
  "telephone": "+91-7000-123456",
  "address": {
    "@type": "PostalAddress",
    "streetAddress": "123 MP Nagar, Zone 2",
    "addressLocality": "Bhopal",
    "addressRegion": "Madhya Pradesh",
    "postalCode": "462011",
    "addressCountry": "IN"
  },
  "geo": {
    "@type": "GeoCoordinates",
    "latitude": "23.2332",
    "longitude": "77.4343"
  },
  "openingHours": "Mo-Sa 09:00-18:00",
  "priceRange": "₹₹"
}
```

#### JSON-LD Example: FAQ

```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "mainEntity": [
    {
      "@type": "Question",
```

### Content Refresh Strategy

Old content loses rankings over time. Refresh strategy:

1. **Identify declining pages** — Check Google Search Console for pages losing clicks/impressions
2. **Update facts and statistics** — Replace outdated data with current numbers
3. **Add new sections** — Cover subtopics that competitors have added
4. **Improve formatting** — Add tables, images, videos, better headings
5. **Update the date** — Change "2024" references to current year
6. **Re-promote** — Share updated content on social media, build new links

### Site Architecture

Good site architecture means:
- Any page is reachable within 3 clicks from homepage
- Clear hierarchy: Homepage → Category → Subcategory → Individual Page
- Flat structure (not too deep)
- Logical URL paths that reflect hierarchy

```
│   └── /blog/social-media-tips/ (Post)
├── /about/
└── /contact/
```

### Crawling & Indexing Controls

#### robots.txt

Located at `yoursite.com/robots.txt`. Tells search engines what to crawl and what to skip.

**Syntax:**
```
User-agent: *          # Applies to all bots
Disallow: /admin/      # Don't crawl admin pages
Disallow: /cart/       # Don't crawl cart pages
Disallow: /thank-you/  # Don't crawl thank you pages
Allow: /blog/          # Explicitly allow blog

User-agent: Googlebot  # Specific rules for Google
Disallow: /tmp/

Sitemap: https://techpath.biz/sitemap.xml
```

**Key rules:**
- `Disallow: /` = block everything
- `Disallow:` (empty) = allow everything
- robots.txt is a suggestion, not a command — malicious bots can ignore it
- Never use robots.txt to hide sensitive pages (use password protection instead)

#### XML Sitemap

A file (`sitemap.xml`) listing all important pages for search engines to find.

**Format:**
```xml
<?xml version="1.0" encoding="UTF-8"?>
<urlset xmlns="http://www.sitemaps.org/schemas/sitemap/0.9">
  <url>
    <loc>https://techpath.biz/</loc>
    <lastmod>2026-07-01</lastmod>
    <changefreq>weekly</changefreq>
    <priority>1.0</priority>
  </url>
  <url>
    <loc>https://techpath.biz/courses/digital-marketing/</loc>
    <lastmod>2026-06-15</lastmod>
    <changefreq>monthly</changefreq>
    <priority>0.8</priority>
  </url>
</urlset>
```

**Best practices:**
- Include only pages you want indexed
- Keep under 50,000 URLs per sitemap (use sitemap index for large sites)
- Submit sitemap in Google Search Console
- Update `lastmod` when content changes

#### Canonical Tags

Tells Google which version of a page is the "original" when duplicates exist.

```html
<link rel="canonical" href="https://techpath.biz/courses/digital-marketing/" />
```

**Use when:**
- Same page accessible via multiple URLs (with/without www, http/https)
- Product pages with color/size parameters
- Paginated content
- Syndicated content (published on multiple sites)

#### Noindex Tag

Tells Google not to show a page in search results (but still crawls it).

```html
<meta name="robots" content="noindex, follow" />
```

**Use for:**
- Thank you pages
- Internal search results
- Tag/author archive pages
- Staging/test pages

**Try This:** Visit any website you like and check these 3 things: (1) Type `yoursite.com/robots.txt` in the browser — what pages are disallowed? (2) Type `yoursite.com/sitemap.xml` — does a sitemap exist? How many URLs are listed? (3) Right-click → View Source on a blog page and search for `canonical` — is a canonical tag present? Does it point to the correct URL? Try this on at least 2 different websites and compare their technical setup.

### Core Web Vitals

Google's page experience metrics. Measured for real users (field data from Chrome User Experience Report).

| Metric | Full Name | What It Measures | Good | Needs Improvement | Poor |
|--------|-----------|-----------------|------|-------------------|------|
| LCP | Largest Contentful Paint | How fast the main content loads | < 2.5s | 2.5s - 4.0s | > 4.0s |
| INP | Interaction to Next Paint | How fast page responds to clicks/taps | < 200ms | 200ms - 500ms | > 500ms |
| CLS | Cumulative Layout Shift | How much content jumps around while loading | < 0.1 | 0.1 - 0.25 | > 0.25 |

**How to improve LCP:**
- Optimize images (compress, use WebP, add width/height)
- Use a CDN (Content Delivery Network)
- Minimize render-blocking CSS/JS
- Preload critical resources

**How to improve INP:**
- Break up long JavaScript tasks
- Use `requestAnimationFrame` for animations
- Minimize main thread work
- Defer non-critical JavaScript

**How to improve CLS:**
- Always set width/height on images and videos
- Don't inject content above existing content
- Use `font-display: swap` for web fonts
- Reserve space for ads and embeds

### Mobile SEO

- Google uses **mobile-first indexing** — it primarily uses the mobile version of your site for ranking
- Use responsive design (single URL that adapts to screen size)
- Test with Google's Mobile-Friendly Test tool
- Ensure tap targets are large enough (48px minimum)
- Don't use intrusive interstitials (popups) on mobile

---

### HTTPS / Security

- HTTPS is a confirmed ranking factor
- Get an SSL certificate (free via Let's Encrypt)
- Redirect all HTTP URLs to HTTPS
- Update internal links to HTTPS
- Fix mixed content warnings (HTTP resources on HTTPS pages)

---

### JavaScript SEO

- Googlebot can render JavaScript, but it's slow and not always reliable
- Use server-side rendering (SSR) or static generation for important content
- Don't hide critical content behind "click to expand" JavaScript
- Ensure navigation links are standard `<a href>` tags (not JavaScript-only)
- Test using "View Rendered Source" in SEO tools

---

### Log File Analysis

Server log files show exactly how Googlebot crawls your site:
- Which pages are crawled most/least frequently
- Response codes Googlebot encounters (200, 301, 404, 500)
- Crawl budget waste (bot crawling unimportant pages)
- Tools: Screaming Frog Log Analyzer, Botify

For each site, note: LCP score, INP score, CLS score, and overall Performance score for both mobile and desktop. Which site scores best? ## Week 14: Off-Page SEO

### Backlinks

A backlink is a link from another website to yours. They are like "votes of trust."

#### Backlink Quality Factors

| Factor | Good | Bad |
|--------|------|-----|
| Domain Authority | High DA (50+) | Low DA (< 10) |
| Relevance | Same industry/topic | Completely unrelated site |
| Anchor Text | Natural, varied | Exact match keyword spam |
| Link Placement | Within content (editorial) | Footer, sidebar, comment |
| Follow Status | Dofollow (passes authority) | Nofollow (no direct SEO benefit) |
| Traffic | Linking page gets real traffic | Dead/no-traffic page |
| Freshness | Recently created | Very old, on dead page |

#### Link Building Methods

| Method | Difficulty | Cost | Quality |
|--------|-----------|------|---------|
| Guest Posting | Medium | Free-₹5,000/post | High |
| HARO/Connectively | Medium | Free | Very High |
| Broken Link Building | Medium | Free | High |
| Digital PR | Hard | ₹10,000-50,000 | Very High |
| Skyscraper Technique | Hard | Free | High |
| Resource Page Links | Easy | Free | Medium |
| Directory Submissions | Easy | Free-₹500 | Low-Medium |

**Guest Posting Process:**
1. Find relevant blogs that accept guest posts (search: "your niche" + "write for us")
2. Study their content style and guidelines
3. Pitch 2-3 unique topic ideas
4. Write a high-quality article (1500+ words)
5. Include 1-2 natural links back to your site

**HARO (Help a Reporter Out) / Connectively:**
1. Sign up as a source
2. Get daily emails with journalist queries
3. Respond with expert quotes within the deadline
4. Get mentioned (with a backlink) in major publications

**Broken Link Building:**
1. Find pages in your niche with broken (404) outbound links
2. Create content that matches what the broken link used to point to
3. Email the site owner: "Hey, I noticed a broken link on your page. I have a similar resource you could link to instead."

**Try This:** Use a free backlink checker (Ahrefs Free Backlink Checker or Ubersuggest) to analyze the backlink profile of 2 websites in the same niche (e.g., two ed-tech companies or two local businesses). Compare: How many total backlinks does each have? What is their Domain Authority? Find 3 sites that link to your competitor but not to the other — those are potential link-building targets. Also, search Google for `"your niche" + "write for us"` and find 3 blogs that accept guest posts.

### Local SEO & Google Business Profile

**NAP Consistency:**
- NAP = Name, Address, Phone Number
- Must be EXACTLY the same everywhere (website, GBP, directories, social media)
- Example: If your GBP says "TechPath Academy, MP Nagar Zone-2, Bhopal" — use that exact format everywhere

**Google Business Profile Optimization:**
1. Claim and verify your listing
2. Choose correct primary + secondary categories
3. Write a detailed description (750 chars, include keywords)
4. Add high-quality photos (interior, exterior, team, work)
5. Add products/services with descriptions and prices
6. Post weekly updates (offers, events, tips)
7. Get reviews — respond to ALL reviews (positive and negative)
8. Add Q&A (and answer your own common questions)
9. Set correct business hours (including special hours)
10. Add booking/appointment link if applicable

**Local Ranking Factors:**
- Proximity (distance from searcher)
- Relevance (how well your listing matches the query)
- Prominence (reviews, citations, links, brand mentions)

### Google Search Console (GSC) Guide

**Key Reports:**

| Report | What It Shows | Action |
|--------|--------------|--------|
| Performance | Clicks, impressions, CTR, position for each query/page | Find quick wins (high impressions, low CTR = improve title tag) |
| Coverage/Pages | Index status (indexed, excluded, errors) | Fix errors, check why pages are excluded |
| Core Web Vitals | LCP, INP, CLS for mobile and desktop | Fix poor URLs first |
| Links | External links, internal links, top linking sites | Monitor backlink growth |
| Manual Actions | If Google has penalized your site | Fix violations, submit reconsideration request |
| Sitemaps | Sitemap submission status | Ensure sitemap is submitted and processing |

**GSC Quick Wins:**
1. Sort by impressions → find keywords where you rank position 8-20 → optimize those pages (easy to push to page 1)
2. Find pages with high impressions but low CTR → rewrite title tag and meta description
3. Check "Excluded" pages → make sure nothing important is accidentally excluded
4. Monitor for crawl errors (404s, server errors) → fix or redirect

## Key Takeaways

- SEO is a long-term game — expect 3-6 months to see results
- Focus on user intent first, keywords second
- Technical SEO is the foundation — fix it before worrying about content
- Quality backlinks > quantity of backlinks
- Track everything in Google Search Console

- Local SEO is critical for businesses with a physical location in India
- Content freshness matters — update old content regularly
