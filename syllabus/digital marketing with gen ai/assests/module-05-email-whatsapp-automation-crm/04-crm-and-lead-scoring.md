## What is CRM and Why It Matters

CRM stands for Customer Relationship Management. It is a system that stores every interaction you have with customers and potential customers — their contact details, what emails they opened, which pages they visited, what they bought, and every conversation you had.

Think of a CRM like a super-powered diary for your business. A diary remembers what you write in it. A CRM remembers everything automatically — and reminds you when to follow up.

**Without CRM (typical small business):**
- Customer details scattered across WhatsApp, emails, Excel sheets, sticky notes
- No one remembers when to follow up with a lead
- Two salespeople contact the same lead, embarrassing the company
- No idea which marketing channel brings the best customers
- When a salesperson leaves, all their customer knowledge leaves with them

**With CRM:**
- Every customer interaction in one place
- Automatic reminders: "Follow up with Rahul in 3 days"
- Lead assigned to one person — no duplicates
- Reports: "Instagram brings 40% of our paying customers"
- New salesperson picks up where the old one left off

> **Real Example:** Freshworks, an Indian SaaS company based in Chennai, built its CRM product (Freshsales) and grew into a ₹15,000 crore+ company listed on NASDAQ. They proved that Indian businesses need affordable, easy-to-use CRM tools — and built exactly that.

## CRM Setup: The Four Core Objects

Every CRM is built around four objects. Understanding these is key to using any CRM tool.

| Object | What It Stores | Example |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **Contact** | Individual person's details | Priya Sharma, priya@company.com, +91-98765-43210 |
| **Company** | Business/organization details | Tata Digital, Mumbai, 500+ employees |
| **Deal** | A potential sale you are tracking | "TechPath Training Package — ₹2,50,000" |
| **Pipeline** | Stages a deal moves through | New → Qualified → Proposal → Negotiation → Won/Lost |

### Pipeline Stages Example

For a digital marketing agency in Delhi:

| Stage | What Happens | Probability |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **New Lead** | Inquiry received, not yet contacted | 10% |
| **Contacted** | Had first call/meeting | 20% |
| **Qualified** | Confirmed budget, need, and timeline | 40% |
| **Proposal Sent** | Shared pricing and scope of work | 60% |
| **Negotiation** | Discussing terms, discounts, changes | 75% |
| **Won** | Contract signed, payment received | 100% |
| **Lost** | Deal did not close (track the reason) | 0% |

> **Fun Fact:** The average sales cycle for a ₹5 lakh B2B deal in India is 45-60 days. Without a CRM tracking pipeline stages, most salespeople forget to follow up after stage 3, losing deals they could have won.

## Lead Scoring Model

Lead scoring assigns points to each lead based on who they are (demographic) and what they do (behavioral). When a lead crosses a threshold score, they are "hot" and ready for sales contact.

### Demographic Scoring (Who They Are)

| Factor | Points | Rationale |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Job title is "Marketing Manager" or above | +15 | Decision maker — can approve purchase |
| Job title is "Intern" or "Student" | -10 | Unlikely to have purchase authority |
| Company has 50+ employees | +10 | Larger budget potential |
| Company has 1-10 employees | +3 | Smaller budget, but still potential |
| Location: Tier-1 city (Mumbai, Delhi, Bangalore) | +5 | Higher spending capacity |
| Location: Tier-2 city (Pune, Jaipur, Lucknow) | +3 | Growing market |
| Industry matches your target (e.g., E-commerce) | +10 | Ideal customer profile |

### Behavioral Scoring (What They Do)

| Action | Points | Rationale |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Visited pricing page | +20 | High purchase intent |
| Downloaded ebook/lead magnet | +10 | Interested in learning more |
| Attended webinar | +20 | Invested significant time |
| Opened 3+ emails in last 30 days | +5 | Actively engaging with content |
| Opened 5+ emails in last 30 days | +10 | Very engaged |
| Clicked a CTA in email | +8 | Taking action, not just reading |
| Visited website 3+ times in a week | +15 | Repeated interest |
| Filled contact form | +25 | Directly asking for information |
| Unsubscribed from email | -20 | Not interested anymore |
| No activity for 60+ days | -15 | Gone cold |

### Scoring Thresholds

| Score Range | Lead Status | Action |
|-------------|------------|--------|
| 0-30 | **Cold** | Keep in email nurture sequence |
| 31-60 | **Warm** | Send targeted content, invite to webinar |
| 61-80 | **Hot (MQL)** | Marketing Qualified — hand to sales for review |
| 80+ | **Very Hot (SQL)** | Sales Qualified — call within 24 hours |

## MQL vs SQL Explained

| Term | Full Form | Meaning | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|---------|
| **MQL** | Marketing Qualified Lead | Has shown enough interest through marketing activities | Downloaded 2 ebooks, attended a webinar, visited pricing page |
| **SQL** | Sales Qualified Lead | Sales team has confirmed they have budget, authority, need, and timeline | Had a discovery call, confirmed ₹2L budget, needs solution by Q3 |

**The handoff process:**
1. Marketing generates leads through content, ads, and campaigns
2. Leads are scored automatically in the CRM
3. When a lead becomes an MQL (score 60+), marketing notifies sales
4. Sales contacts the lead, qualifies them further
5. If the lead has BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline), they become an SQL
6. Sales creates a deal in the CRM pipeline and works to close it

**Lead routing rules:**
- Score 80+ AND visited pricing page → Assign to senior sales rep
- Score 60-80 → Assign to next available sales rep
- Industry = "EdTech" → Assign to Raj (EdTech specialist)
- Location = "South India" → Assign to Chennai office team

## AARRR — Pirate Metrics Framework

AARRR (pronounced "Arrr!" like a pirate) is a framework by Dave McClure that measures the complete customer journey in 5 stages. Every startup and marketing team in India should track these metrics.

### 1. Acquisition — Where Do Users Come From?

| Channel | What to Measure | Tools |
|---------|----------------|-------|
| Google Search (SEO) | Organic sessions, keyword rankings | GA4, Google Search Console |
| Social Media | Followers, profile visits, link clicks | Instagram Insights, LinkedIn Analytics |
| Paid Ads | Cost per click (CPC), impressions | Google Ads, Meta Ads Manager |
| Referral | Referral traffic sources | GA4 → Acquisition → Traffic Acquisition |
| Direct | People typing your URL directly | GA4 |

**How to improve:** Double down on the channel with the lowest cost per acquisition (CPA). If Instagram brings leads at ₹50 each and Google Ads at ₹200 each, increase Instagram spend first.

### 2. Activation — Do They Have a Great First Experience?

| What to Measure | Target | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| Signup completion rate | >60% | 100 people start signup, 65 complete it |
| Time to first value | <5 minutes | User creates their first project within 5 minutes |
| Onboarding completion | >50% | At least half finish the tutorial/walkthrough |

**How to improve:** Simplify signup (remove unnecessary fields), add a guided onboarding tour, show a quick win in under 2 minutes.

### 3. Retention — Do They Come Back?

| What to Measure | Target | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| Day 1 retention | >40% | 40% of new users return the next day |
| Day 7 retention | >20% | 20% still active after a week |
| Day 30 retention | >10% | 10% still active after a month |
| Churn rate | <5%/month | Less than 5% of customers leave each month |

**How to improve:** Email/WhatsApp reminders, push notifications, regular new content, loyalty rewards, community building.

> **Real Example:** Cred's retention strategy is genius — they send monthly credit card bill reminders (useful), reward you with Cred coins for paying bills (gamification), and run exclusive deals (value). This keeps users coming back even though bill payment apps are a commodity.

### 4. Revenue — Do They Pay?

| What to Measure | Target | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| Conversion rate (free → paid) | >2-5% | 3 out of 100 free users upgrade |
| Average revenue per user (ARPU) | Depends on product | ₹500/month per user |
| Customer lifetime value (LTV) | >3x CAC | If acquisition costs ₹500, LTV should be ₹1,500+ |

**How to improve:** Offer limited free trial (not freemium forever), show ROI/value clearly, add social proof, optimize pricing page.

### 5. Referral — Do They Tell Others?

| What to Measure | Target | Example |
|----------------|--------|---------|
| Net Promoter Score (NPS) | >50 | Customers rate you 9-10 out of 10 |
| Referral rate | >10% | 1 in 10 customers refers a friend |
| Viral coefficient | >0.5 | Each user brings at least 0.5 new users |

**How to improve:** Referral programs ("Give ₹500, Get ₹500"), shareable content, word-of-mouth triggers, amazing customer experience.

> **Real Example:** Razorpay grew largely through referrals. When a developer integrates Razorpay, they recommend it to other developers. Razorpay's referral program gives ₹500 credit per referral — a tiny cost compared to ₹5,000+ spent on acquiring a customer through ads.

## Net Promoter Score (NPS)

NPS measures how likely customers are to recommend your business to others. It is the simplest and most popular customer satisfaction metric.

**The NPS question:** "On a scale of 0-10, how likely are you to recommend us to a friend?"

| Score | Category | What It Means |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| 9-10 | **Promoters** | Love your product, will refer others |
| 7-8 | **Passives** | Satisfied but not enthusiastic |
| 0-6 | **Detractors** | Unhappy, may leave negative reviews |

**NPS Formula:** NPS = % Promoters - % Detractors

**Example:** 100 responses → 60 Promoters, 25 Passives, 15 Detractors
NPS = 60% - 15% = **45** (Good score; above 50 is excellent)

## Summary

- CRM stores **every customer interaction** in one place — no more scattered WhatsApp chats and Excel sheets
- **HubSpot CRM** is free and the best starting point for beginners
- CRM is built around four objects: **Contacts, Companies, Deals, and Pipelines**
- Lead scoring assigns **demographic points** (who they are) and **behavioral points** (what they do)
- **MQL** is marketing-qualified (score 60+); **SQL** is sales-qualified (budget, authority, need, timeline confirmed)
- **AARRR Pirate Metrics** track the full customer journey: Acquisition → Activation → Retention → Revenue → Referral
- Improve each AARRR stage: find the **cheapest acquisition channel**, simplify **activation**, send reminders for **retention**, optimize pricing for **revenue**, build referral programs for **referral**
- **NPS** (Net Promoter Score) is the simplest way to measure customer satisfaction — ask one question, get one number
- Indian companies like **CRED, Flipkart, and Zomato** use loyalty programs with points, tiers, and subscription models
- Start with a **free CRM** today — even managing 10 leads in a CRM is better than managing 1,000 in your head
