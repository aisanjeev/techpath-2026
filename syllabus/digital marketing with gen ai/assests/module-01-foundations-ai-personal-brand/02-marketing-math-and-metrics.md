## Why Marketing Math Matters

Marketing without measurement is like driving with your eyes closed. Every rupee you spend on marketing should be tracked, measured, and optimized. In this class, you will learn the formulas that every digital marketer uses daily.

> **Pro Tip:** In job interviews, companies will ask you to calculate ROAS or CAC on the spot. Knowing these formulas sets you apart from candidates who only know "posting on Instagram."

---

## The 10 Essential Marketing Metrics

### 1. CTR — Click-Through Rate

**What it measures:** Out of everyone who SAW your ad/email/post, how many CLICKED on it?

**Formula:**

```
CTR = (Clicks / Impressions) x 100
```

**Example:** Your Google Ad was shown 10,000 times. 250 people clicked on it.

```
CTR = (250 / 10,000) x 100 = 2.5%
```

| Platform | Good CTR (India) |
|----------|-----------------|
| Google Search Ads | 3-5% |
| Google Display Ads | 0.5-1% |
| Facebook/Instagram Ads | 1-3% |
| Email Marketing | 2-5% |
| LinkedIn Ads | 0.5-1.5% |

> **Pro Tip:** A low CTR usually means your ad headline or image is not compelling enough. The fix is better creative, not more budget.

---

### 2. CPC — Cost Per Click

**What it measures:** How much you pay each time someone clicks your ad.

**Formula:**

```
CPC = Total Ad Spend / Total Clicks
```

**Example:** You spent ₹5,000 on Facebook Ads and got 200 clicks.

```
CPC = ₹5,000 / 200 = ₹25 per click
```

| Platform | Average CPC (India) |
|----------|---------------------|
| Google Search Ads | ₹15-80 |
| Facebook/Instagram Ads | ₹5-30 |
| LinkedIn Ads | ₹50-200 |
| YouTube Ads | ₹2-10 (per view) |

> **Real Example:** An education startup in Bangalore running Google Ads for "data science course" might pay ₹80-150 per click because education keywords are very competitive in India.

---

### 3. CPM — Cost Per Mille (Cost Per 1,000 Impressions)

**What it measures:** How much you pay for 1,000 ad views (not clicks — just views).

**Formula:**

```
CPM = (Total Ad Spend / Total Impressions) x 1,000
```

**Example:** You spent ₹3,000 and your ad was shown 50,000 times.

```
CPM = (₹3,000 / 50,000) x 1,000 = ₹60
```

| Platform | Average CPM (India) |
|----------|---------------------|
| Facebook/Instagram | ₹40-150 |
| YouTube | ₹30-100 |
| Google Display | ₹20-80 |
| LinkedIn | ₹200-600 |

---

### 4. CPA — Cost Per Acquisition

**What it measures:** How much you pay to acquire ONE customer (or one lead, one signup, one sale).

**Formula:**

```
CPA = Total Ad Spend / Total Conversions
```

**Example:** You spent ₹20,000 on Instagram Ads. 40 people signed up for your free trial.

```
CPA = ₹20,000 / 40 = ₹500 per acquisition
```

> **Pro Tip:** CPA is the metric that CFOs care about. They do not care about likes or followers — they care about "How much did it cost to get a paying customer?"

---

### 5. Conversion Rate

**What it measures:** Out of everyone who visited your website (or landing page), how many completed the desired action (purchase, signup, download)?

**Formula:**

```
Conversion Rate = (Conversions / Total Visitors) x 100
```

**Example:** 5,000 people visited your online store. 150 people made a purchase.

```
Conversion Rate = (150 / 5,000) x 100 = 3%
```

| Industry | Good Conversion Rate (India) |
|----------|------------------------------|
| E-commerce | 1-3% |
| SaaS / Software | 3-7% |
| Education / Courses | 5-10% |
| Lead generation (B2B) | 2-5% |
| Landing pages (optimized) | 5-15% |

---

### 6. Bounce Rate

**What it measures:** The percentage of visitors who land on your page and leave WITHOUT clicking anything else.

**Formula:**

```
Bounce Rate = (Single-page visits / Total visits) x 100
```

**Example:** Out of 8,000 visitors to your blog, 4,800 left without clicking any other page.

```
Bounce Rate = (4,800 / 8,000) x 100 = 60%
```

| Page Type | Acceptable Bounce Rate |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Blog post | 60-80% (normal — people read and leave) |
| Landing page | 30-50% |
| Home page | 30-50% |
| E-commerce product page | 20-40% |

> **Pro Tip:** A high bounce rate on your landing page means the page does not match what the ad promised. Fix the mismatch between ad copy and landing page content.

---

### 7. CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost

**What it measures:** The TOTAL cost of getting one new customer — including all marketing AND sales expenses.

**Formula:**

```
CAC = Total Marketing + Sales Expenses / Number of New Customers
```

**Example:** In one month, you spent ₹1,00,000 on ads, ₹50,000 on content creation, and ₹30,000 on a sales executive's salary. You acquired 60 new customers.

```
CAC = (₹1,00,000 + ₹50,000 + ₹30,000) / 60 = ₹3,000 per customer
```

> **Real Example:** Swiggy's estimated CAC for a new user is ₹150-300 (through app install campaigns + first-order discounts). For Swiggy One subscriptions, it is higher — around ₹500-800.

---

### 8. LTV — Lifetime Value (also called CLV — Customer Lifetime Value)

**What it measures:** The total revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship with your business.

**Formula (simple):**

```
LTV = Average Order Value x Purchase Frequency x Customer Lifespan
```

**Example:** A customer orders from your online store:
- Average order: ₹800
- Orders 3 times per year
- Stays a customer for 4 years

```
LTV = ₹800 x 3 x 4 = ₹9,600
```

**The Golden Rule: LTV should be at least 3x your CAC.**

| Scenario | CAC | LTV | LTV:CAC Ratio | Verdict |
|----------|-----|-----|---------------|---------|
| Healthy | ₹500 | ₹3,000 | 6:1 | Excellent — scale up! |
| Acceptable | ₹1,000 | ₹3,000 | 3:1 | Good — keep optimizing |
| Dangerous | ₹2,000 | ₹3,000 | 1.5:1 | Warning — reduce CAC |
| Bleeding money | ₹4,000 | ₹3,000 | 0.75:1 | Stop and fix immediately |

---

### 9. ROAS — Return on Ad Spend

**What it measures:** For every ₹1 you spend on ads, how much revenue do you get back?

**Formula:**

```
ROAS = Revenue from Ads / Ad Spend
```

**Example:** You spent ₹25,000 on Google Ads. Those ads generated ₹1,50,000 in sales.

```
ROAS = ₹1,50,000 / ₹25,000 = 6x (or 600%)
```

| Industry | Good ROAS (India) |
|----------|-------------------|
| E-commerce | 3x-5x |
| D2C Brands | 2x-4x |
| SaaS | 5x-10x |
| Education / Courses | 4x-8x |
| Local businesses | 3x-6x |

> **Pro Tip:** A ROAS of 1x means you are breaking even on ad spend (not accounting for product costs, salaries, etc.). Most businesses need at least 3x ROAS to be profitable.

---

### 10. ROI — Return on Investment

**What it measures:** The overall profitability of your entire marketing effort (not just ads — everything).

**Formula:**

```
ROI = ((Revenue - Total Marketing Cost) / Total Marketing Cost) x 100
```

**Example:** Total marketing spend for the quarter: ₹5,00,000. Revenue generated from marketing: ₹15,00,000.

```
ROI = ((₹15,00,000 - ₹5,00,000) / ₹5,00,000) x 100 = 200%
```

This means for every ₹1 spent, you earned ₹2 in profit (after recovering the marketing cost).

**Time:** 10 minutes

Read each scenario aloud. Students calculate on paper or calculator. Discuss answers as a class.

1. You spent ₹10,000 on ads and got 500 clicks. What is your CPC?
2. Your website got 2,000 visitors. 80 people bought something. What is your conversion rate?
3. Your ad was shown 25,000 times and got 375 clicks. What is your CTR?
4. You spent ₹50,000 on marketing and got 25 new customers. What is your CAC?
5. A customer spends ₹1,200 per order, orders 4 times a year, for 3 years. What is their LTV?

**Answers:** 1. ₹20   2. 4%   3. 1.5%   4. ₹2,000   5. ₹14,400

## Case Study: Chai pe Charcha Cafe

**Background:** Chai pe Charcha is a small cafe in Indore that wants to grow using digital marketing. Here is their marketing data for one month:

| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| Total marketing budget | ₹50,000 |
| Google Ads spend | ₹20,000 |
| Instagram Ads spend | ₹15,000 |
| Content creation (photographer + designer) | ₹10,000 |
| Influencer collaboration (1 food blogger) | ₹5,000 |
| Google Ads impressions | 80,000 |
| Google Ads clicks | 2,400 |
| Instagram Ads impressions | 1,20,000 |
| Instagram Ads clicks | 3,600 |
| Website visitors (total) | 6,000 |
| Online orders placed | 180 |
| Average order value | ₹350 |
| New customers acquired | 120 |
| Revenue from marketing | ₹1,05,000 |

**Let us calculate all metrics:**

**Google Ads CTR:**
```
CTR = (2,400 / 80,000) x 100 = 3%   (Good for search ads)
```

**Google Ads CPC:**
```
CPC = ₹20,000 / 2,400 = ₹8.33 per click   (Excellent for a local business)
```

**Instagram Ads CTR:**
```
CTR = (3,600 / 1,20,000) x 100 = 3%   (Good for social ads)
```

**Instagram Ads CPC:**
```
CPC = ₹15,000 / 3,600 = ₹4.17 per click   (Very good)
```

**Google Ads CPM:**
```
CPM = (₹20,000 / 80,000) x 1,000 = ₹250
```

**Overall Conversion Rate:**
```
Conversion Rate = (180 / 6,000) x 100 = 3%   (Good for a cafe)
```

**CAC:**
```
CAC = ₹50,000 / 120 = ₹417 per customer
```

**LTV (assuming customer orders 2x/month for 12 months):**
```
LTV = ₹350 x 2 x 12 = ₹8,400
```

**LTV:CAC Ratio:**
```
₹8,400 / ₹417 = 20:1   (Excellent!)
```

**Overall ROAS (on ad spend only):**
```
ROAS = ₹1,05,000 / ₹35,000 = 3x   (Healthy)
```

**Overall ROI:**
```
ROI = ((₹1,05,000 - ₹50,000) / ₹50,000) x 100 = 110%   (For every ₹1 spent, ₹1.10 profit)
```

**Verdict:** Chai pe Charcha is doing well. Instagram Ads have a lower CPC, so they should shift more budget there. Their LTV:CAC ratio of 20:1 is excellent — they can afford to spend more on customer acquisition.

## When Each Metric Matters Most

| Metric | When to Focus On It |
|--------|-------------------|
| **CTR** | When testing ad creatives or email subject lines |
| **CPC** | When managing ad budgets — want cheap clicks |
| **CPM** | When running brand awareness campaigns |
| **CPA** | When optimizing for leads or signups |
| **Conversion Rate** | When improving your website or landing page |
| **Bounce Rate** | When people visit but leave immediately |
| **CAC** | When presenting to investors or management |
| **LTV** | When planning long-term pricing and retention strategies |
| **ROAS** | When deciding which ad campaigns to scale or kill |
| **ROI** | When reporting overall marketing effectiveness |

---

## Common Mistakes in Marketing Math

| Mistake | Why It Is Wrong | Correct Approach |
|---------|----------------|-----------------|
| Celebrating high CTR with no sales | Clicks without conversions waste money | Track conversions, not just clicks |
| Ignoring LTV when setting CAC | You might cut profitable campaigns | Always compare CAC to LTV |
| Comparing CPM across platforms | Different platforms have different audiences | Compare CPA instead |
| Reporting ROAS without including all costs | ROAS ignores content, salary, tools costs | Use ROI for the full picture |
| Using vanity metrics (followers, likes) | They do not pay bills | Focus on revenue-driving metrics |

## Summary

- **CTR** = Clicks / Impressions x 100 — measures ad or content appeal
- **CPC** = Spend / Clicks — measures cost efficiency of paid campaigns
- **CPM** = (Spend / Impressions) x 1,000 — measures awareness campaign cost
- **CPA** = Spend / Conversions — measures cost of acquiring a lead or customer
- **Conversion Rate** = Conversions / Visitors x 100 — measures website effectiveness
- **Bounce Rate** = Single-page visits / Total visits x 100 — measures page relevance
- **CAC** = Total Marketing Cost / New Customers — measures total acquisition cost
- **LTV** = Avg Order x Frequency x Lifespan — measures customer value over time
- **ROAS** = Revenue / Ad Spend — measures ad profitability
- **ROI** = (Revenue - Cost) / Cost x 100 — measures overall marketing profitability
- The **golden rule**: LTV should be at least **3x CAC**
- Always focus on **revenue-driving metrics**, not vanity metrics
