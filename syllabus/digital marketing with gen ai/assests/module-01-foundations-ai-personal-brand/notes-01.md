### Day 2 — How Digital Marketing Makes Money

Every business exists to make profit. Digital marketing is the engine that brings in customers profitably. Today, we learn the math that separates successful marketers from failed ones.

#### The Core Business Math

**CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost**
```
CAC = Total Marketing Spend / Number of New Customers

Example: Rahul runs a coaching institute in Jaipur.
He spent ₹1,00,000 on Google Ads last month.
He got 50 new student enrollments.
His CAC = ₹1,00,000 / 50 = ₹2,000 per student.
```

**LTV — Lifetime Value (how much a customer is worth over time)**
```
LTV = Average Order Value x Purchase Frequency x Customer Lifespan

Example: Priya runs an online saree store.
Average order = ₹3,500
Customers order 3 times per year
Average customer stays for 4 years
LTV = ₹3,500 x 3 x 4 = ₹42,000

This means each customer is worth ₹42,000 over their lifetime.
So she can afford to spend up to ₹14,000 to acquire one customer 
(keeping the 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio).
```

**ROAS — Return on Ad Spend**
```
ROAS = Revenue from Ads / Cost of Ads

Example: Ananya's D2C skincare brand "GlowUp"
Ad spend on Meta Ads: ₹2,00,000
Revenue from those ads: ₹8,00,000
ROAS = ₹8,00,000 / ₹2,00,000 = 4x

For every ₹1 spent, she made ₹4 back.
Industry benchmark: 3x-5x ROAS is considered good for D2C.
```

**ROI — Return on Investment**
```
ROI = (Revenue - Total Cost) / Total Cost x 100

Example: Vikram's digital marketing agency
Revenue from a client campaign: ₹5,00,000
Total cost (ads + team + tools): ₹2,00,000
ROI = (₹5,00,000 - ₹2,00,000) / ₹2,00,000 x 100 = 150%
```

#### The Profitability Rule

```
GOLDEN RULE: LTV must be at least 3x CAC

If LTV = ₹42,000 and CAC = ₹10,000 → Healthy (4.2x ratio)
If LTV = ₹42,000 and CAC = ₹20,000 → Risky (2.1x ratio)
If LTV = ₹42,000 and CAC = ₹50,000 → You are LOSING money
```

#### Other Key Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Good Benchmark |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| CPC (Cost Per Click) | Ad Spend / Clicks | ₹5-50 for India |
| CTR (Click-Through Rate) | (Clicks / Impressions) x 100 | 2-5% is good |
| CPM (Cost Per 1000 Impressions) | (Ad Spend / Impressions) x 1000 | ₹50-300 for India |
| Conversion Rate | (Conversions / Visitors) x 100 | 2-5% for e-commerce |
| Bounce Rate | Single-page visits / Total visits | Below 60% is good |
| CLTV:CAC Ratio | LTV / CAC | 3:1 or higher |

#### Worked Example: Is This Business Profitable?

```
Ananya runs "FitBox" — a healthy meal subscription in Bengaluru.

Monthly subscription: ₹4,000
Average customer stays: 6 months
LTV = ₹4,000 x 6 = ₹24,000

Marketing spend this month: ₹3,00,000
New customers this month: 80
CAC = ₹3,00,000 / 80 = ₹3,750

LTV:CAC ratio = ₹24,000 / ₹3,750 = 6.4x

Verdict: VERY HEALTHY. She can afford to spend even more on ads.
```

### Day 6 — Camera Confidence Workshop

If you want to succeed in digital marketing, you WILL need to be on camera. Reels, YouTube videos, LinkedIn video posts, client presentations — video is everywhere. Today we conquer camera fear.

#### The Technical Setup (Budget-Friendly)

**Lighting:**
- Best free option: Sit facing a window during daytime (natural light on your face)
- Budget option: ₹500-1000 ring light from Amazon
- Key rule: Light should fall ON your face, not behind you

**Framing (Rule of Thirds):**
- Position your eyes in the upper third of the frame
- Leave a small gap between your head and the top of the frame
- Show from chest/shoulders up for talking-head videos

**Audio:**
- Phone mic is fine for starting, but avoid noisy rooms
- Budget upgrade: ₹500-800 collar mic (lavalier) from Amazon
- Record in a room with curtains/soft furnishings (reduces echo)

**Background:**
- Clean, uncluttered wall or bookshelf
- Avoid messy rooms, moving people, bright windows behind you
- A simple bedsheet pinned to the wall works as a clean backdrop

#### Overcoming Camera Fear

**The truth:** Nobody is naturally confident on camera. Every YouTuber you admire was terrible in their first 20 videos. The only way to get better is to record, watch, cringe, and record again.

**The 30-Second Hook Formula:**
```
Opening line structure for any video:
"Did you know that [surprising fact]? In this video, I will show you 
[what they will learn] in just [time]. Let's go."

Example:
"Did you know that 90% of Indian startups fail because of bad marketing? 
In this video, I will show you the 3 biggest mistakes to avoid — 
in under 2 minutes. Let's go."
```

#### Practical Tips

1. **Look at the camera lens**, not your face on screen
2. **Speak as if you are talking to ONE friend**, not a crowd
3. **Use the 3-second rule:** After hitting record, pause for 3 seconds, take a breath, then start
4. **Stand up** if possible — it gives you more energy
5. **Use hand gestures** — they make you look more natural
6. **Record 10 bad videos before expecting 1 good one**
7. **Do not script word-for-word** — use bullet points and speak naturally

#### Energy Levels for Different Platforms

| Platform | Energy Level | Style |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| YouTube (long-form) | Medium-High | Conversational, like explaining to a friend |
| Instagram Reels | Very High | Quick, punchy, lots of energy in first 2 seconds |
| LinkedIn Video | Medium | Professional but warm, like a work conversation |
| Course Videos | Calm-Medium | Clear, steady, educational |

### Day 9 — Prompt Engineering

The quality of your AI output depends 80% on the quality of your input (prompt). Bad prompt = bad output. Great prompt = great output. Today you learn to write prompts like a professional.

#### The CRAFT Framework

```
C — CONTEXT:  Background information the AI needs
R — ROLE:     What expert persona should the AI adopt
A — ACTION:   The specific task you want done
F — FORMAT:   How you want the output structured
T — TONE:     The voice and style of the output
```

**Example 1 — Social Media Caption:**

**1. Few-Shot Prompting (show examples)**
```
Write product descriptions in this style:
```

**2. Chain-of-Thought (break complex tasks into steps)**
```

Step 2: Identify 3-5 content pillars

```

**3. Role-Playing (for different perspectives)**
```

Role 2: A marketing professor who evaluates copy technically
Role 3: A Gen-Z student who scrolls fast and has a 3-second attention span

Give me feedback from each perspective separately.
```

| Mistake | Bad Prompt | Better Prompt |

### Day 13 — Buyer Personas with AI

You cannot market to everyone. The more specifically you define who your customer is, the more effectively you can reach them. A buyer persona is a fictional but research-backed profile of your ideal customer.

#### What Makes a Good Persona

A persona is NOT just demographics (age, gender, location). The best personas include:

1. **Demographics** — Age, location, income, education, job
2. **Psychographics** — Values, beliefs, interests, lifestyle, personality
3. **Jobs-to-be-Done** — What are they trying to achieve in life? What problem are they hiring your product to solve?
4. **Pain points** — What frustrates them?
5. **Buying behavior** — Where do they research? What triggers a purchase? What stops them?

#### The Jobs-to-be-Done Framework

"People do not buy products. They hire products to do a job."

```
Example: Why does someone buy a gym membership in January?

Surface answer: "To get fit"
JTBD answer: "To feel confident at my cousin's wedding in March"

Example: Why does someone enroll in a digital marketing course?

Surface answer: "To learn marketing"
JTBD answer: "To get a job that pays ₹5 LPA+ within 6 months 
so I can stop depending on my parents"
```

Understanding the REAL job helps you write better marketing messages.

#### Persona Template (Complete)

```
PERSONA NAME: Rahul Verma

DEMOGRAPHICS:
- Age: 22
- Gender: Male
- Location: Indore, Madhya Pradesh
- Income: ₹0 (dependent on parents, family income ~₹8 LPA)

PSYCHOGRAPHICS:
```

### Day 14 — Customer Journey Mapping

No customer goes from "I have never heard of you" to "Take my money" in one step. The customer journey has stages, and your job as a marketer is to create the right content for each stage.

#### The 5 Stages of the Customer Journey

```
Stage 1: AWARENESS — "I have a problem/need"
         ↓
Stage 2: CONSIDERATION — "What are my options?"
         ↓
Stage 3: DECISION — "Which one should I pick?"
         ↓
Stage 4: RETENTION — "Was this worth it? Should I continue?"
         ↓
Stage 5: ADVOCACY — "This was great, everyone should try it!"
```

#### Detailed Breakdown

| Stage | Customer Thinking | Content to Create | Channels | Metric to Track |
|-------|------------------|-------------------|----------|-----------------|
| Awareness | "I need to learn a skill to get a job" | Blog posts, Reels, YouTube videos, infographics | Instagram, YouTube, Google Search | Impressions, Reach, Traffic |
| Consideration | "Should I learn digital marketing or data analytics?" | Comparison posts, webinars, free workshops, guides | Email, YouTube, LinkedIn | Email signups, Time on page |
| Decision | "TechPath or competitor X? Is it worth ₹25K?" | Testimonials, case studies, free trial, placement stats | WhatsApp, Website, Remarketing ads | Demo bookings, Inquiries |
| Retention | "Am I learning enough? Is this working?" | Onboarding emails, community, progress tracking, support | WhatsApp group, Email, App | Completion rate, NPS score |
| Advocacy | "My friend should join too!" | Referral program, alumni features, UGC campaigns | WhatsApp, LinkedIn, Instagram | Referral count, Reviews |

#### Journey Map Example: "Priya Enrolls in TechPath Academy"

```
WEEK 1 (Awareness):
- Priya sees a reel on Instagram: "5 skills that pay ₹50K/month without coding"
- She watches, likes, follows the page
- She sees more content over the next few days

WEEK 2 (Consideration):
- She clicks the link in bio, reads a blog: "Digital Marketing vs Data Analytics — Which to Choose?"
- She signs up for a free webinar
- She attends the webinar and downloads the syllabus PDF

WEEK 3 (Decision):
- She joins a WhatsApp group for "interested students"
- She reads testimonials from past students
- She has a call with a counselor who answers her objections
- She enrolls using EMI option

MONTH 1-6 (Retention):
- She receives a welcome email sequence
- She joins the student community group
- She completes weekly assignments and gets feedback
- Monthly progress calls keep her motivated

AFTER COURSE (Advocacy):
- She posts her placement news on LinkedIn
- She refers 2 friends and gets ₹2,000 cashback each
- She becomes a student ambassador
```


### Day 17 — Marketing Funnel Frameworks

A funnel is a visual model of how strangers become customers. Different frameworks exist — learn all of them, then pick the one that fits your situation best.

#### TOFU / MOFU / BOFU (The Standard Funnel)

```
        ┌─────────────────────────────┐
        │         TOFU                │  ← Many people (thousands)
        │   Top of Funnel             │
        │   (Awareness)               │
        │   Blog, Reels, YouTube      │
        ├─────────────────────────────┤
        │       MOFU                  │  ← Fewer people (hundreds)
        │   Middle of Funnel          │
        │   (Consideration)           │
        │   Emails, Webinars, Guides  │
        ├─────────────────────────────┤
        │     BOFU                    │  ← Fewest (tens)
        │   Bottom of Funnel          │
        │   (Decision)                │
        │   Demos, Calls, Offers      │
        └─────────────────────────────┘
```

**Content for each stage:**

| Stage | Goal | Content Types | Call to Action |
|-------|------|---------------|----------------|
| TOFU | Attract attention | Reels, blog posts, carousels, YouTube videos | Follow, Subscribe, Visit website |
| MOFU | Nurture interest | Email sequences, webinars, case studies, free tools | Sign up, Download, Register |
| BOFU | Convert to customer | Testimonials, demos, limited offers, consultations | Buy, Enroll, Book a call |

#### AIDA Framework

| Letter | Meaning | Your Task | Example |
|--------|---------|-----------|---------|
| A | Attention | Grab their attention in 2 seconds | Bold headline, surprising statistic |
| I | Interest | Make them want to know more | Relatable problem, interesting story |
| D | Desire | Make them want YOUR solution | Benefits, testimonials, results |
| A | Action | Tell them exactly what to do next | "Enroll now," "Book a free demo" |

**Example ad using AIDA:**
```
[Attention] "I went from ₹0 to ₹3L/month in 8 months."
[Interest] "After BCA, nobody was hiring. I wasted 6 months applying 
to 200+ jobs. Then I learned digital marketing..."
[Desire] "Now I manage ads for 4 clients from my hometown. No commute, 
no boss breathing down my neck. TechPath taught me exactly how."
[Action] "Want the same? DM 'START' for the course details."
```

#### RACE Framework (for Planning)

| Stage | Meaning | Activities | KPI |
|-------|---------|-----------|-----|
| R | Reach | Get found (SEO, social media, PR) | Impressions, unique visitors |
| A | Act | Get engagement (content, UX) | Page views, time on site, leads |
| C | Convert | Get sales (offers, CTAs) | Sales, sign-ups, revenue |
| E | Engage | Build loyalty (email, community) | Repeat purchases, referrals, NPS |

#### See-Think-Do-Care (Google's Framework)

| Stage | Audience | Content | Metric |
|-------|----------|---------|--------|
| See | Largest possible audience with no intent | Brand awareness, entertainment | Reach, impressions |
| Think | People starting to consider options | Educational content, comparisons | Engagement, clicks |
| Do | People ready to buy | Product pages, offers, reviews | Conversions, revenue |
| Care | Existing customers | Loyalty programs, support, community | Retention, LTV, referrals |

### Day 18 — Goal Setting (SMART, OKRs, KPIs)

"What gets measured, gets managed." If you do not set clear goals and track them, you are just posting content and hoping for the best.

#### SMART Goals Framework

| Letter | Meaning | Question to Ask | Bad Example | Good Example |
|--------|---------|----------------|-------------|--------------|
| S | Specific | What exactly will I do? | "Grow on social media" | "Post 5 Reels per week on Instagram" |
| M | Measurable | How will I know I succeeded? | "Get more followers" | "Reach 2,000 Instagram followers" |
| A | Achievable | Can I realistically do this? | "Get 1 million followers in a month" | "Grow by 500 followers per month" |
| R | Relevant | Does this align with my bigger goals? | "Learn cooking" | "Learn content creation for my marketing career" |
| T | Time-bound | When is the deadline? | "Someday" | "By September 30, 2026" |

**Example SMART Goals for a Digital Marketing Student:**
```
1. "I will publish 20 LinkedIn posts by August 31, 2026, documenting 
   my learning journey, to build visibility with recruiters."

2. "I will complete 3 client projects (even if free) by October 31, 2026, 
   to have a portfolio for job applications."

3. "I will reach 1,000 YouTube subscribers by December 31, 2026, by 
   posting 2 tutorial videos per week consistently."
```

#### OKRs (Objectives & Key Results)

OKRs are how companies like Google, Zerodha, and Swiggy set goals.

```
OBJECTIVE: A qualitative, inspiring goal
KEY RESULTS: 3-5 measurable outcomes that prove you achieved the objective

Example 1:
Objective: Build a strong personal brand in digital marketing

Key Results:
- KR1: Publish 30 LinkedIn posts with average 50+ likes each
- KR2: Reach 1,000 LinkedIn connections in marketing/tech
- KR3: Get featured in 2 newsletters or podcasts
- KR4: Receive 5 inbound messages from recruiters or potential clients

Example 2:
Objective: Become proficient in AI-powered content creation

Key Results:
- KR1: Master 5 AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Midjourney, ElevenLabs, Runway)
- KR2: Create a 10-piece AI-generated portfolio
- KR3: Reduce content creation time by 50% compared to manual methods
- KR4: Get 1 paid project using AI-assisted content
```

#### The 12 KPIs Every Marketer Must Track

| # | KPI | What It Tells You | Target Benchmark |
|---|-----|-------------------|-----------------|
| 1 | Website Traffic | How many people visit your site | Growth month-over-month |
| 2 | Bounce Rate | % who leave without interacting | Below 60% |
| 3 | Conversion Rate | % who take desired action | 2-5% (industry avg) |
| 4 | CAC | Cost to acquire one customer | Below LTV/3 |
| 5 | LTV | Total value of a customer over time | 3x+ CAC |
| 6 | ROAS | Revenue per rupee spent on ads | 3x-5x |
| 7 | Email Open Rate | % who open your emails | 20-25% |
| 8 | Email CTR | % who click links in email | 2-5% |
| 9 | Social Engagement Rate | (Likes+Comments+Shares) / Followers x 100 | 3-6% |
| 10 | Follower Growth Rate | New followers / Total followers x 100 | 5-10% monthly |
| 11 | Content Reach | Unique accounts who saw your content | Growing trend |
| 12 | Revenue/Lead | Revenue attributed to marketing | Positive ROI |

### Day 22 — Twitter/X & Threads Strategy

Twitter (now X) and Threads are where real-time conversations happen. Marketers, founders, and industry leaders share raw, unfiltered thoughts here. If LinkedIn is the conference room, X is the coffee shop.

#### Tweet Anatomy

```
Character limit: 280 characters (free) / 25,000 (Premium)
Best tweet length: 70-140 characters (short and punchy)

Structure:
[Hook/claim/question] + [insight/proof] + [CTA or punchline]

Example:
"The best marketers I know do not post daily.
They post when they have something worth saying.
Quality > quantity. Always."
```

#### Thread Architecture (Multi-tweet Posts)

Threads are powerful — they let you share long-form ideas while keeping the engagement of short posts.

```
Tweet 1 (HOOK): "I analyzed 100 viral marketing campaigns in India.
Here are the 7 patterns they ALL share: 🧵"

Tweet 2: Pattern 1 — They solve a real problem (explain + example)
Tweet 3: Pattern 2 — They use emotion (explain + example)
Tweet 4: Pattern 3 — They are shareable (explain + example)
...
Tweet 8: Pattern 7 — They have a clear CTA (explain + example)

Tweet 9 (CTA): "If this was helpful:
1. Repost the first tweet to help others
2. Follow me @handle for more marketing insights
3. Reply with which pattern surprised you most"
```

#### Growth Tactics for X

1. **Reply to big accounts** — Add value to their tweets (not just "agreed!")
2. **Quote tweet with insight** — Add your take to viral tweets
3. **Post at peak times** — 8-9 AM, 12-1 PM, 9-10 PM IST
4. **Use 1-2 hashtags max** (unlike Instagram, too many looks spammy on X)
5. **Engage before you post** — Spend 15 minutes engaging, THEN post your tweet
6. **Pin your best tweet** — First impression for new profile visitors

#### Threads (by Meta) — The LinkedIn Killer?

Threads is newer but growing fast. Key differences from X:
- 500 character limit
- No hashtags (uses topics instead)
- Audience from Instagram carries over
- More positive/conversational tone
- Less news, more personal/professional content

**Threads strategy:**
- Cross-post your LinkedIn content (with slight tone adjustment)
- More casual than LinkedIn, more professional than Instagram
- Great for building community and having conversations

## Quick Reference: Key Formulas & Frameworks

### Marketing Math Cheat Sheet

```
CAC = Total Marketing Spend / New Customers Acquired
LTV = Avg Order Value x Purchase Frequency x Customer Lifespan
ROAS = Revenue from Ads / Cost of Ads
ROI = (Revenue - Cost) / Cost x 100
CPC = Ad Spend / Total Clicks
CTR = (Clicks / Impressions) x 100
CPM = (Ad Spend / Impressions) x 1000
Conversion Rate = (Conversions / Total Visitors) x 100
Engagement Rate = (Likes + Comments + Shares) / Followers x 100

GOLDEN RULE: LTV should be at least 3x CAC
```
