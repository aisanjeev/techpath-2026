# AI Prompt Library for Digital Marketers (CRAFT Framework)

> Each prompt follows the CRAFT framework: **C**ontext, **R**ole, **A**ction, **F**ormat, **T**one.
> Replace items in [brackets] with your specific details.

---

## Category 1: SEO (Search Engine Optimization)

### Prompt 1 — Keyword Research
```
Context: I run a [business type] in [city, India] targeting [audience]. My website is new with low domain authority.
Role: You are an SEO specialist with 10 years of experience in local SEO for Indian businesses.
Action: Generate 20 long-tail keywords I can realistically rank for in the next 3 months. Group them by search intent (informational, commercial, transactional).
Format: Table with columns: Keyword | Search Intent | Estimated Difficulty (Low/Medium) | Content Type to Create.
Tone: Practical and data-driven.
```

### Prompt 2 — Blog Post Outline for SEO
```
Context: I want to rank for the keyword "[your keyword]" targeting [audience] in India. My competitors' top articles are about 1500 words.
Role: You are an SEO content strategist who writes for E-E-A-T compliance.
Action: Create a detailed blog post outline with H2 and H3 headings, suggested word count per section, and 3 internal linking opportunities.
Format: Hierarchical outline with word counts. Include a meta title (under 60 chars) and meta description (under 155 chars).
Tone: Professional yet easy to read for Indian readers.
```

### Prompt 3 — Local SEO Google My Business
```
Context: I manage a [business type] located in [area, city]. We serve customers within a 10km radius. Our services include [list services].
Role: You are a local SEO expert specializing in Google Business Profile optimization.
Action: Write an optimized business description (750 characters), suggest 10 relevant categories, and create 5 Google Posts for this month.
Format: Numbered sections for each element. Google Posts should have a headline, body (under 300 words), and CTA.
Tone: Friendly and local — like a trusted neighbourhood business.
```

## Category 2: Social Media Marketing

### Prompt 4 — Instagram Caption Generator
```
Context: I run a [brand/business] account on Instagram. My audience is [age group] in [city/India]. This post is about [topic/product].
Role: You are a social media copywriter who creates viral Instagram captions for Indian audiences.
Action: Write 5 caption variations for this post. Each should have a strong hook (first line), valuable body, and clear CTA. Include 15 relevant hashtags.
Format: Number each caption. Separate hashtags at the end. Mark the hook line in bold.
Tone: Conversational, use Hinglish where natural, keep it relatable for young Indians.
```

### Prompt 5 — LinkedIn Post Series
```
Context: I am a [your role/student] building my personal brand on LinkedIn. My content pillars are [list 3 topics]. I want to post 5 times this week.
Role: You are a LinkedIn content strategist who has helped creators grow from 0 to 10K followers.
Action: Create 5 LinkedIn posts for this week following the 4-1-1 rule (4 value, 1 promo, 1 personal). Each post should be 150-200 words with a scroll-stopping first line.
Format: Label each as [VALUE], [PROMO], or [PERSONAL]. Include emoji suggestions and posting day.
Tone: Professional but human. First-person narrative.
```

### Prompt 6 — YouTube Video Script
```
Context: I have a YouTube channel about [niche] with [X] subscribers. This video is about "[title]" targeting [audience] who want to [goal].
Role: You are a YouTube scriptwriter who understands Indian viewer retention patterns.
Action: Write a full script for an 8-minute video. Include: hook (first 30 seconds), intro, 3 main points with examples, and CTA. Add timestamp markers.
Format: Screenplay format with [VISUAL] cues and [B-ROLL] suggestions. Include a suggested thumbnail text.
Tone: Energetic, educational, like explaining to a friend. Use simple English.
```

## Category 3: Ad Copy (Paid Advertising)

### Prompt 7 — Google Search Ad Copy
```
Context: I am running Google Ads for a [business type] in [city]. Budget is ₹[X]/day. Target keyword is "[keyword]". Landing page offers [service/product] at ₹[price].
Role: You are a Google Ads copywriter with experience in Indian markets.
Action: Write 5 responsive search ad variations with 15 headlines (30 chars max each) and 4 descriptions (90 chars max each). Include 3 sitelink extensions.
Format: Group headlines and descriptions separately. Label character counts.
Tone: Direct, benefit-focused, urgency-driven.
```

### Prompt 8 — Facebook/Instagram Ad Copy
```
Context: I am promoting [product/service] priced at ₹[price] targeting [audience] in [location]. The campaign objective is [awareness/leads/sales].
Role: You are a Meta Ads copywriter who writes high-converting ad copy for Indian D2C brands.
Action: Write 3 ad copy variations using different frameworks: (1) PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution, (2) AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action, (3) Story-based testimonial style.
Format: Label each framework. Include primary text, headline, and description for each. Suggest 3 CTA button options.
Tone: Conversational, relatable, creates FOMO without being pushy.
```

### Prompt 9 — Retargeting Ad Copy
```
Context: A visitor came to my [product/service] page but did not buy. They spent [X] seconds on the page. My product costs ₹[price] and solves [problem].
Role: You are a performance marketer specializing in retargeting funnels.
Action: Write 3 retargeting ad copies that address common objections: price, trust, and urgency. Each should remind them of the value without being annoying.
Format: Short ad copy (under 125 words each). Include a headline and one-line description.
Tone: Warm, understanding, with a gentle nudge. Not salesy.
```

## Category 4: Email Marketing

### Prompt 10 — Welcome Email Sequence
```
Context: A new subscriber just joined my email list for [lead magnet/offer]. My brand is [brand name], and we help [audience] achieve [goal]. I sell [product/service] at ₹[price].
Role: You are an email marketing expert who builds nurturing sequences for Indian startups.
Action: Write a 5-email welcome sequence. Email 1 (Day 0): Deliver lead magnet + introduce brand. Email 2 (Day 2): Share a valuable tip. Email 3 (Day 4): Social proof/case study. Email 4 (Day 6): Soft pitch. Email 5 (Day 8): Hard CTA with urgency.
Format: For each email: Subject line, preview text, body (under 200 words), CTA button text.
Tone: Friendly, helpful, builds relationship before selling.
```

### Prompt 11 — Promotional Email
```
Context: I am sending a promotional email for [product/offer]. Discount is [X]% valid for [Y] days. My email list has [audience type] subscribers. Average open rate is [Z]%.
Role: You are a conversion copywriter who specializes in email campaigns for Indian e-commerce.
Action: Write 3 subject line options (A/B test) and the full email body. Include urgency, social proof, and a clear CTA. Also write a follow-up email for non-openers (different subject line, same offer).
Format: Label clearly: Subject Lines → Email Body → Follow-up Email.
Tone: Excited but not spammy. Use power words.
```

### Prompt 12 — Cart Abandonment Email
```
Context: A customer added [product] (₹[price]) to their cart on my [store type] but did not complete purchase. Common reasons for abandonment in my category are [list reasons].
Role: You are an e-commerce email strategist.
Action: Write a 3-email cart abandonment sequence sent at 1 hour, 24 hours, and 72 hours. Each should address a different objection.
Format: For each: Subject line, body (under 150 words), CTA. Email 3 should include a small incentive.
Tone: Helpful and understanding, not desperate.
```

## Category 5: Content Marketing

### Prompt 13 — Blog Post Writer
```
Context: I need a blog post on "[topic]" for my website targeting [audience]. The primary keyword is "[keyword]" and secondary keywords are [list]. My brand voice is [describe].
Role: You are a content writer who follows E-E-A-T guidelines and writes for Indian readers.
Action: Write a 1500-word blog post with proper H2/H3 headings, 2-3 examples from Indian context, and an FAQ section (4 questions). Naturally place keywords without stuffing.
Format: Full article in markdown with headings, bold key points, and a bullet summary at the end.
Tone: Educational, simple English, conversational but authoritative.
```

### Prompt 14 — Content Repurposing
```
Context: I have a blog post about "[topic]" that is [X] words long. Here is the main content: [paste key points or full text]. I want to repurpose it across platforms.
Role: You are a content strategist who maximizes ROI from every piece of content.
Action: Repurpose this into: (1) 5 Twitter/X posts, (2) 1 LinkedIn carousel outline (10 slides), (3) 3 Instagram captions, (4) 1 YouTube Shorts script (60 seconds), (5) 5 email newsletter snippets.
Format: Organize by platform. Each piece should stand alone without needing the original blog.
Tone: Adapt tone for each platform (professional for LinkedIn, casual for Instagram, punchy for Twitter).
```

## Category 6: Brand Strategy

### Prompt 16 — Brand Voice Guide
```
Context: I am building a brand called [name] in the [industry] space targeting [audience] in India. Our competitors are [list 2-3]. We want to stand out by being [differentiator].
Role: You are a brand strategist who has built voice guides for Indian D2C brands.

```

### Prompt 17 — Brand Positioning
```
Context: I am launching [product/service] in [city/India]. My target customers are [describe]. Direct competitors are [list]. My price point is ₹[X] compared to competitors at ₹[Y-Z].

Tone: Strategic, clear, jargon-free.
```

## Category 8: Persona Building

### Prompt 22 — Detailed Buyer Persona
```
Context: I sell [product/service] at ₹[price] targeting [broad audience] in [city/region]. My best customers so far have been [describe any patterns you have noticed].
Role: You are a consumer psychologist who builds buyer personas for Indian brands.
Action: Create 2 detailed buyer personas with: Indian name, age, city, income, job, family status, goals, frustrations, media habits, purchase triggers, objections, and preferred communication channels.
Format: Card-style format for each persona. Include a "day in the life" paragraph and a "how to reach them" summary.
Tone: Vivid and specific — make these feel like real people.
```

### Prompt 23 — Pain Point Discovery
```
Context: My target audience is [describe]. They are trying to [goal] but struggle with [broad area]. I want to understand their specific frustrations so I can address them in my marketing.
Role: You are a customer research expert who understands middle-class Indian consumer psychology.
Action: List (1) 10 specific pain points this audience faces, (2) the emotional impact of each pain point, (3) the exact words/phrases they would use to describe each frustration, (4) which pain point is most urgent (and why).
Format: Table with columns: Pain Point | Emotional Impact | Their Words | Urgency Level.
Tone: Empathetic, research-backed.
```

### Prompt 24 — Customer Journey Map
```
Context: A potential customer wants to buy [product/service] from me. They are a [persona description] in [city]. The buying process typically takes [timeframe].
Role: You are a customer experience designer mapping the Indian buyer journey.
Action: Map the complete customer journey from "unaware of problem" to "loyal advocate." For each stage, identify: (1) what they think/feel, (2) what they search/do, (3) touchpoints with my brand, (4) content I should create, (5) potential drop-off points.
Format: Table or stage-by-stage breakdown. Include 6 stages: Unaware → Aware → Considering → Deciding → Buying → Advocating.
Tone: Detailed and actionable.
```

## Category 10: Analytics & Reporting

### Prompt 28 — Monthly Report Template
```
Context: I manage digital marketing for [brand/client]. Platforms used: [list]. Monthly budget is ₹[X]. Key goals are [list KPIs].
Role: You are a digital marketing analyst who creates client-ready reports.
Action: Create a monthly report template with sections for: (1) Executive summary, (2) Channel-wise performance (metrics + analysis), (3) Top-performing content, (4) Budget utilization, (5) Key learnings, (6) Next month plan.
Format: Professional report structure with placeholder tables and suggested charts.
Tone: Professional, data-driven, insight-focused (not just numbers).
```

### Prompt 29 — Campaign Post-Mortem
```
Context: I just completed a [campaign type] for [brand/product]. Duration: [X days]. Budget: ₹[Y]. Results: [share key metrics]. Target was [what you aimed for].
Role: You are a performance marketing analyst.
Action: Analyze this campaign and provide: (1) Was it successful? (compare results vs targets), (2) What worked well (top 3 factors), (3) What underperformed (top 3 issues), (4) Specific recommendations for the next campaign, (5) Budget reallocation suggestions.
Format: Structured analysis with clear sections. Use numbers and percentages.
Tone: Honest, analytical, constructive.
```

### Prompt 30 — KPI Dashboard Setup
```
Context: I am setting up tracking for a [business type] that uses [platforms/tools]. Business goals are: [list 3 goals]. Current tools available: Google Analytics, platform insights, [any others].
Role: You are a marketing analytics consultant helping small Indian businesses measure what matters.
Action: Define (1) 5 primary KPIs with targets, (2) 10 secondary metrics to track, (3) recommended tracking frequency for each, (4) a simple dashboard layout, (5) which free tools to use for each metric.
Format: Table format with: Metric | Target | Frequency | Tool | Why It Matters.
Tone: Practical, beginner-friendly, focused on actionable metrics not vanity metrics.
```

## How to Use This Library

1. **Pick the prompt** closest to your task
2. **Fill in the brackets** with your specific information
3. **Paste into ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini** and hit enter
4. **Review the output** — it will never be perfect on the first try
5. **Iterate** — ask follow-up questions to refine the output
6. **Customize** — add your own context and examples for better results

### Pro Tips
- Combine prompts: Use Prompt 22 (Persona) output as input for Prompt 4 (Captions)
- Save your best prompts in a Notion database for reuse
- A/B test outputs from ChatGPT vs Claude for different tasks
- Always fact-check data, statistics, and claims before publishing
